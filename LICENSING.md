original: existing upstream notices and specifically identified exceptions take
precedence over the general matrix below.

The public converter grant is **AGPL-3.0-only**, not “or later.” The full text is
in `src/go2cs/LICENSE` and `LICENSES/AGPL-3.0-only.txt`. The copyright holders
grant one additional permission under AGPL section 7: the **go2cs Converter
Output Exception** in `src/go2cs/LICENSE-EXCEPTION`, which states that the
converter's output is never a covered work merely because go2cs produced it and
carries the MIT grant for the embedded templates and other original scaffolding
reproduced in that output. Every converter source header refers to it, as
section 7 requires.

Alternative commercial licensing for the converter is available from the
copyright holder listed in `AUTHORS`, at the contact address given there. No
commercial contract or additional public commercial grant is supplied here, and
`Commercial` is not an SPDX identifier used by this repository.

Applicable third-party rights and notices remain in effect.

## Generated output

Code generated by go2cs is not subject to the go2cs converter's AGPL license merely
because it was produced by go2cs. Rights in generated output remain governed by the
rights applicable to the input source and any separately licensed components
incorporated into or referenced by the output. `src/go2cs/LICENSE-EXCEPTION` states
this as a binding additional permission.

Original output scaffolding supplied by the project (including emitted project
files, package metadata, adapters and test hosts) is available under MIT for user
applications, through the same exception. Project-owned implementations and
scaffolding distributed within the standard-library packages are available under
BSD-3-Clause. The MIT grant covers the scaffolding emitted into applications, not
the converter's implementation or conversion algorithms. MIT runtime, shared
symbols and generator use does not require an application to adopt AGPL;
applicable MIT notice obligations still apply. No license is inferred for
arbitrary user source.

Converted Go standard-library packages retain their applicable upstream licensing.
There is no additional license grant for the act of conversion. Their NuGet README
states that upstream license. Packaging reads `src/core/LICENSE` through a
relative path and places it at `LICENSE` inside the archive, without creating
copies in individual project folders. Project-owned handwritten additions within
these packages use the same BSD-3-Clause license and shared license file.

## NuGet packaging options

`-license "MIT"` or `-license "BSD-3-Clause OR MIT"` explicitly selects NuGet
`PackageLicenseExpression` metadata. NuGet validates supported SPDX expressions
when packing. This option does not change rights in input source or remove
existing notices. It overrides generated or custom-template license metadata.

Without an override, a local `LICENSE` is selected as `PackageLicenseFile` when it
exists. Known standard-library projects otherwise select the shared upstream file.
The third-party dependency modules of a `-recurse` conversion select their own
module's license file (`LICENSE`, `LICENSE.md`, `LICENSE.txt`, `LICENCE*` or
`COPYING*`, matched case-insensitively), which conversion copies verbatim to the
converted module's root once per module and every package of the module packs by
relative path, exactly as standard-library packages pack `src/core/LICENSE`. The
application's own module and a dependency that ships no such file are left
unspecified, with one warning per module on stderr, rather than guessed. A local
license added later is picked up at packing time. License expressions and
file-based license metadata are not emitted together; a local license can still be
included as a resource with an explicit expression. Application projects resolve
the same metadata (a local `LICENSE` when present, or the override) but warn about
nothing, since an application is not packed by default.

Local `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and `AUTHORS` files are packed only if present. Conversion
never authors license, notice or author text; the only license file it writes is
the verbatim copy of a dependency module's own file described above. Generated
standard-library READMEs carry artwork credits and attribution/license links.
End-user projects may supply their own local notice or README attribution for
included artwork.

## Provenance and exceptions

Keep original copyright holders and years. Do not replace Go Authors notices with
go2cs notices. `src/core/LICENSE` records the license shipped with the converted
source tree, with the go2cs Authors added as a copyright holder on its second line
for the project-owned BSD-licensed additions that share the file. The converted
tree's `VERSION` records its Go release. Regeneration copies attribution from the
selected source tree and inserts that second line deterministically, so the
committed file is what a regeneration writes.

Hand edits to Go-derived standard-library code retain **BSD-3-Clause**, including
manual fixes, adaptations and handwritten translations. Preserve the upstream
copyright and license notices: hand maintenance does not make derived code
original. This rule applies throughout the converted standard library, including
platform-specific files and upstream-derived tests.

Project-owned original implementations and support files within standard-library
packages also use BSD-3-Clause. Their headers credit The go2cs Authors; upstream
copyright holders and notices remain attached to derived code. This policy covers
handwritten replacements, additions and tests, and the hand-owned `unsafe` and
`testing` packages, whose READMEs say so. Third-party license requirements take
precedence where applicable. The separate runtime (`src/core/golib/`), shared
symbols (`src/core/go2cs/`) and Roslyn generators use MIT.

The behavioral fixtures `Constraints`, `FirstClassFunctions`, `Solitaire` and
`StdLibInternalAbi` contain Go-derived sources. Their local `LICENSE` files carry
BSD terms alongside MIT terms for separately authored go2cs additions. Their
source files and behavioral goldens follow their respective provenance and notices:
the converter emits a source file's recognized copyright and license notices into
its output even without `-comments`, so those goldens begin with the Go notice.

The gopher and go2cs icon attribution in `NOTICE` remains applicable. The original
documentation's MIT license does not override these image licenses or licenses on
quoted/adapted third-party material.

## Header policy

Converter Go files use `SPDX-License-Identifier: AGPL-3.0-only`, refer to the
converter's `LICENSE`, and carry the notice line that AGPL section 7 requires for
an additional permission:

```text
// <code file name> - Gbtc
// Copyright © 2026 The go2cs Authors. All rights reserved.
//
// SPDX-License-Identifier: AGPL-3.0-only
// Use of this source code is governed by the GNU Affero General Public License
// version 3 only, which can be found in the LICENSE file.
// Additional permission for emitted output: see LICENSE-EXCEPTION (AGPL section 7).
```

Original MIT-licensed code outside the converter uses an MIT-style header:

```text
// <code file name> - Gbtc
// Copyright © 2026 The go2cs Authors. All rights reserved.
//
// Use of this source code is governed by an MIT-style license
// that can be found in the LICENSE file.
```

Project-owned files inside the converted standard library use the same shape with
`SPDX-License-Identifier: BSD-3-Clause` and "a BSD-style license". These examples
do not replace existing authors or years. Upstream-derived files keep upstream
headers instead. `LICENSES/` stores full texts; the root `LICENSE` provides a
scoped MIT grant for files with MIT headers. Local MIT license files in runtime,
symbols and generator directories keep those components from inheriting the
surrounding BSD or converter terms. The converter's own test suite
(`TestLicensing*`) checks every converter header, every corpus header and every
corpus package's license metadata, and the `Licensing boundaries` workflow runs
those checks on master and on pull requests.

`-provenance` optionally identifies a converted file's source. It defaults to off,
contains no conversion date/time and uses portable paths. Required recognized
source license notices are preserved independently of this option. Unknown source
licenses remain unknown. No blanket SPDX declaration is added to user output.

## Ownership and contributions

Copyright in original go2cs material is held by the authors listed in the
repository's one authoritative `AUTHORS` file. Project-owned MIT notices link to
that list on GitHub. Packages do not search for or require it; they include an
`AUTHORS` resource only if one exists beside the project file.

Portions of go2cs are written with AI assistance (Claude, by Anthropic) working
under the direction of the listed author, who reviews and accepts the results. An
AI tool is not an author or a copyright holder: the provider's terms assign its
output to the user who directs it, and such output has no independent claimant.
Those contributions are owned by the directing author. The assistance is recorded
in commit trailers as attribution, not as a claim of ownership.

Contributions to the AGPL-licensed converter are accepted only with a Developer
Certificate of Origin sign-off (`Signed-off-by`) and a perpetual, irrevocable,
transferable grant letting the copyright holder, and the holder's successors and
assigns, sublicense the contribution under any terms, made explicitly once as a
pull-request comment that the `CLA Assistant` workflow records in
`.github/cla-signatures.json`. Contributors keep the copyright in their own lines;
the grant is what keeps alternative commercial licensing and an acquisition
possible without their further consent and without any share of the proceeds
attaching to their lines. A contribution offered without it is declined or kept
out of the converter. Contributions to the MIT and BSD components need only the
sign-off, since those licenses already permit sublicensing. `CONTRIBUTING.md`
states both; the `DCO` workflow checks the sign-off on every pull request, and the
`CLA Assistant` workflow asks for the grant on every converter pull request and
blocks it until the grant is made.
