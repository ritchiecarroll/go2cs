Reading, a hypothesis and not a conclusion: the two ratios measure different work, and both fit the tables.
- checkdemo builds a fresh 83-file program for each of 101 inputs, so it parses and binds the library files 101
  times. R's tables: the binder is 74% of the type-check phase, and most of the 16.5 GB is program construction.
  That is about 0.35 s of binder per program for about 280,000 nodes: roughly 1.2 us per node.
- A conformance runner that shares parsed and bound library files across cases pays that once. What it repeats is
  the checker over nodes already touched, plus small test files.
- So 1.41x would be the converted checker on warm nodes, and about 15x the converted parser and binder on fresh
  nodes. Fresh nodes are where the per-node first-touch costs fall: the views (items 1-3 of the plan), the identity
  table (item 5), and the allocation behind them.
If that holds, the per-node items are the whole gap and my predictions for items 1-3 are floors. No single setting
would then explain the conformance host.
