# P3 design review: G M:N pin b3dc12ddbb BLOCK on one point (idle retire rule counts pinned carriers: stranded step with K >= P), else sound; 4 SHOULD
from: P3 · to: COORD · 2026-11-20T19:11:05Z

P3 design review: G's M:N pin, claude/g-mn-proto-b1 b3dc12ddbb, docs/phase4/DESIGN-mn-pin.md, read against src/core/golib/runtime/GoScheduler.cs at that commit. Read-only.

Verdict: BLOCK on one point (finding 1, a hang the K = 2P arm cannot see). The cure is a one-line rule. With it, section 3 and L1-L5 are the right pin to build; L2 named and not built is fine.

The wake racing the park commit: sound. m_pin is written before slot.Commit, and both ways a wake can arrive pass through an interlocked operation on the slot after that write (Commit's own "already woken" branch at :54, or Wake's Exchange at :44), so Ready always reads the pin. A wake that lands before the carrier reaches its private wait is kept, because the wake is a permit and not a pulse. One goroutine has one pending step, so one step field suffices.

Findings:

1. BLOCK, GoScheduler.cs:633 (the retire rule), unstated in section 3.2 and 5. An idle shared carrier retires when `s_carriers.Count > s_procs`. Pinned carriers stay in s_carriers, so with K >= P pinned parked goroutines every shared carrier retires after 100 ms idle. Scenario, P = 2, K = 2: both shared carriers time out of s_work.Wait(100) (:625) and s_idle is 0; a wake enqueues a step; Enqueue calls EnsureCarrier (:266), which finds room = 4 - 2 < 2 false (:384) and adds nothing; both carriers then take the lock, see Count 4 and 3 over 2, and return. The step sits in the queue with no carrier until some later Enqueue. A 100 ms ticker with K >= P pinned goroutines arrives near that window on every tick. Plain blocks share the path today, but those threads come back; a pin does not. M3's K = 2P arm runs a continuous ping-pong, never idles 100 ms, and cannot see it. Minimal fix: retire on the unblocked count (`Count - s_blocked > s_procs`, read under the lock), state it in 3.2, and add an arm: K = 2P pinned parked, work arriving every 100 ms and every 250 ms, completes, with carriers-started flat after warm-up.

2. SHOULD-FIX, section 3.1: a fourth step-end case is missing. Locked, the step ended, no park pending and the goroutine not ended (it awaited something that is not a twin or a park, L5). If the carrier pins and waits, the resume runs off-carrier (:203) and the carrier waits forever. Say what happens: refuse by name, or route that resume to the pinned carrier.

3. SHOULD-FIX, section 3.4: state the order as an invariant: m_pin is cleared BEFORE the commit on unpin. Cleared after, a wake between the two is delivered to a carrier that has left its private wait, and the goroutine never runs. Add it to the made-to-fail list.

4. SHOULD-FIX, section 6-7: the bands. P2 predicts 1.6-3.0 us with a stop line at about 3.4: the prediction covers everything short of the stop line, so it can only miss by stopping. It does not separate "as on today's path" (M5's wording, about 1.7) from 1.75 times it. Give a mechanism estimate (one thread wake, one twin wake, one P acquire: about 1.6-2.1) and keep 2x as the stop line. P3 likewise (thread path plus two P acquires and two swaps).

5. SHOULD-FIX, an unpriced cost of 3.3. Go hands the waker's P to the locked thread. Here the pinned carrier waits in s_p.Wait with every other waiter, so with all P inside long steps its wake waits for a step to end. For code that locks (GUI and audio loops) that latency is what matters, and M2 measures an unsaturated ping-pong only. Add an arm: pinned wake latency with P CPU-bound twins running, with a stop line.

6. NIT, size. "golib only" holds on my read: runtime's LockOSThread already mirrors the count, and no signature moves. The line count is likely low once findings 1-3 are in (130-180); not a concern.
