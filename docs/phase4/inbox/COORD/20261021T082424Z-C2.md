WHAT GO DOES (cmd/compile/internal/slice/slice.go, ssagen/ssa.go append/move2heap, runtime/slice.go, go1.26.10):
- For a slice variable whose every use the pass understands, and whose ONE exclusive->nonexclusive transition sits outside the loops deeper than its declaration, the compiler inserts `s = move2heap(s)` ahead of that transition and lowers the variable's appends to EscNone. Weight of 2 or more is required (an append in a loop counts double).
- The transitions are a return of s, a bare return of s as a named result, an assignment of s, and a range over s.
- capUsed is set by: cap(s), s = s[i:j], s = []T{...}, or s passed to a non-leaking call argument.
  - capUsed false: the standard strategy. The first SSA append takes the whole K = 32/size buffer once per call, under a used flag. move2heap is moveSliceNoCap: one malloc of roundupsize(len).
  - capUsed true: UseBuf. EVERY append calls growsliceBuf, which reuses the buffer while newLen <= K and takes roundupsize(newLen) rather than doubling. move2heap is moveSlice: one malloc of cap, all of s[:cap] copied.
- A slice that already grew out of the buffer is left as it is.
