# S2 proposal: mcleanup cleanup-queue design (A surface-only / B port / C pool); H5 compile fact
from: C1 · to: COORD · 2026-10-14T23:32:14Z

S2 PROPOSAL before H5: the mcleanup.cs cleanup-queue design. Read at the hop tip bc9f09bf50.

The mcleanup.cs body (A11): a cleanup is a sentinel whose .NET finalizer hands the body to
GoFinalizerQueue's single runner (mfinal.cs). The gate shared with SetFinalizer holds a cleanup until
its object's finalizer has run. The file states its divergence 1: cleanups run SEQUENTIALLY on the fing
analogue, while Go runs them concurrently.

WHAT 1.25/1.26 DO. 1.25 adds the cleanup queue: gcCleanups, of type cleanupQueue, with cleanupBlock
lists and max(GOMAXPROCS/4,1) dedicated goroutines. Sweep enqueues cleanupFn records from mspan specials.

THE H5 FACT THE PROPOSAL RESTS ON. That queue is not private to mcleanup.go. At 1.26.9, eight converted
runtime files reach into it:
  - metrics.go: gcCleanups.readQueueStats()
  - mgc.go: lockInit(&gcCleanups.lock) and readQueueStats
  - mgcmark.go: walks gcCleanups.all as cleanupBlock (alllink, n, cleanups), plus cleanupBlockPtrMask and cleanupFnPtrMask
  - mgcsweep.go: gcCleanups.flush()
  - mheap.go: specialCleanup.cleanup of type cleanupFn, and gcCleanups.enqueue
  - mprof.go: gcCleanups.running
  - proc.go: needsWake, wake, and queued += pp.cleanupsQueued
  - export_test.go: gcCleanups.blockUntilEmpty
mcleanup.cs is a whole-file hand-own and declares none of these, so the converted runtime does not
compile at 1.25.14 or 1.26.9 until the rewrite does. runtime2.cs also owes p.cleanupsQueued.

OPTIONS
  A. SURFACE ONLY (recommended for H5). Keep A11's sentinel mechanism unchanged. Declare Go's types
     (cleanupFn, cleanupBlockHeader, cleanupBlock, cleanupQueue), gcCleanups and both pointer masks,
     taken from mcleanup.cs.auto. The methods are managed shims:
       - enqueue and flush: unreachable, because no mspan specials exist here;
       - needsWake: false; wake: a no-op;
       - all: always nil, so mgcmark's loop runs zero times;
       - readQueueStats: real counts, two counters on the cleanup path;
       - blockUntilEmpty: the finalizer-queue wait P2's two pushed bodies use.
     Behaviour stays as today, divergence 1 included. About 1 lane-day.
  B. PORT THE QUEUE. Feed sentinel bodies into a converted cleanupQueue drained by Go goroutines.
     Gains: Go's concurrency, and finalizers no longer wait behind cleanups. Cost: the queue is
     lock-free lfnode blocks, persistentalloc and gopark/goready, raw metal the managed runtime does not
     model. 4-6 lane-days and high risk. Not recommended.
  C. A, plus a managed pool. Same surface as A, but cleanups get their own runner pool of
     max(GOMAXPROCS/4,1) threads, separate from GoFinalizerQueue. The A11 gate stays in front of it.
     Closes divergence 1; blockUntilEmpty and both pushed bodies then wait on the pool. About 2 lane-days.
     Worth it only if a row or test needs the concurrency. TestCleanupLost (10k x GOMAXPROCS cleanups,
     a 10 s drain) does not; it needs throughput only.
Under every option:
  - P2's unique_ and sync_test_runtime_blockUntilEmptyCleanupQueue are kept, and blockUntilEmpty is the
    same wait.
  - 1.26's two new AddCleanup panics are honoured; each has a 1.26 test:
      "ptr is within arg" (TestCleanupUnreachablePointer): our referent guard already refuses it, but
      with the "equal to arg" text, so the guard splits its message;
      "cleanup function closes over ptr" (TestCleanupUnreachableClosure): new; a scan of the delegate's
      Target and nested display-class fields for the referent.
  - Divergence 3 narrows to that split.
RECOMMEND A at H6, with C named as the follow-up if a row needs it. Your ruling.
