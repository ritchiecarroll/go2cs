SIZING (no cut): aot-smoke osx-x64 against the 6-hour hosted limit. Options, costs, and one recommendation.

WHAT THE LEG IS. aot-smoke publishes one small consumer with PublishAot=true on macos-15-intel (GitHub's standard Intel image; its core count I have not read off a log). go.lib's default
TrimMode=partial roots every referenced go.* assembly WHOLE, so ILC compiles the full 31-package closure, go.runtime included.
That closure is the leg's distinct value. Release-smoke's E-publish-symbols-aot already Native-AOT publishes a small fixture
on BOTH mac chips in minutes (PASS on all four legs at 1.24.13.7, run 38525428410).

READINGS, from records and published bytes (nothing new run):
- osx-x64 wall: FL 16293 s, T3 16345 s, T3 landing 16377 s, T4 > 21528 s (cancelled at 6h, killed during ld/clang).
  Before T4 it already used 85% of the cap. A gate at 85-100% of a hard cap fails whenever the runner is slow; T4 only
  exposed that.
- AOT input, IL bytes of the 31 closure assemblies, published 1.24.13.6 to 1.24.13.7, osx-x64 asset or lib/:
  13,521,920 to 13,897,216 (+2.8%). Nearly all of it is go.runtime: 2,679,296 to 2,990,080 (+11.6%); every other
  assembly moved by 0-6 KB. +2.8% IL against at least +31% wall does not settle (a) runner drift against (b) T4 growth.
  ILC time is not linear in IL, so the IL figure cannot be read as a wall prediction.

OPTIONS:
(1) MEASURE ILC BY PHASE, locally, same box. Publish the aot-smoke consumer for linux-x64 against nuget 1.24.13.6, then
    1.24.13.7, one after the other on this 4-core box, each with -bl. The binlog splits ILC compile from the native link,
    and the pair attributes the T4 delta with no runner noise. Cost: about 2 x 1.5-2 h of box time, no hosted minutes,
    roughly 6 GB scratch, purged after. It settles (a) against (b) and says which phase grew.
(2) SPLIT IT. Not possible inside the leg: one PublishAot is one ILC invocation, and the 6 h cap is per job and cannot be
    raised on hosted runners. Splitting by RID is already done.
(3) REDUCE ITS PROGRAM. The consumer is already minimal; its cost is the whole-closure compile. A TrimMode=full consumer
    would cut ILC work sharply, but it would stop testing the shipped default (partial, the trim-default seat).
    Acceptable only as an extra MEASURED arm, never as the gate's replacement.
(4) BIGGER RUNNER. A larger Intel macOS runner (GitHub lists 12-core "-large" labels) is billed per minute. ILC codegen parallelizes, so I expect 2-3x,
    unmeasured. This needs the owner's billing decision.
(5) BOUND IT EXPLICITLY. Run the osx-x64 publish under its own 5 h 30 m cap inside the step. On expiry, print
    "NOT MEASURED (timeout): osx-x64 publish exceeded 5h30m" and exit 0, so the job ends readable instead of cancelled.
    linux-x64 and win-x64 stay the AOT gates. Darwin AOT stays covered by E-aot on both chips. About 15 lines in
    os-matrix.yml; no runner cost. It loses only the darwin whole-closure compile, and only on slow days.

RECOMMENDATION: (5) now, plus (1) to decide whether anything more is owed.
(5) makes the leg's failure mode a reading rather than a 6 h cancel that takes the run's conclusion with it. It keeps
every gate that has held (linux and win AOT, both mac E-aot).
(1) decides the rest. If 1.24.13.7 is materially slower than .6 on the same box, T4 grew ILC cost, and that is a finding
for G and P2's seats, not a CI problem; I would name the phase. If it is not, the hosted fleet drifted and (5) is the whole
answer. (4) only if the owner wants darwin whole-closure AOT to gate.
