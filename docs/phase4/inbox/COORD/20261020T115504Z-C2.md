WHY THE HAND-OWN CANNOT JUST "LOCK": it is not Go's mechanism. godebug.cs keeps no per-name cache and no update().
It re-parses $GODEBUG lazily on the first Value() after the text changes ("with no such hook here"). Go's lock is
per cached name and happens at the eager update on each os.Setenv. A lazy port cannot see an intermediate $GODEBUG:
Setenv(fips140=on), then Setenv(fips140=off), then a first New("fips140").Value() reads "on" in Go and would read
"off" in a lazy emulation.
SHAPE I PROPOSE (B): port Go's cache and update() verbatim, and register update the way Go does.
- The runtime already has Go's whole hook. godebug_setUpdate is the linkname target of internal/godebug.setUpdate,
  and godebugNotify runs from syscall_runtimeSetenv/Unsetenv, passing godebugDefault (= GoDefaultGodebugAttribute,
  goenvs_impl.cs) and the stored godebugEnv.
- The hand-own reaches it as it already reaches setNewIncNonDefault: a [ModuleInitializer] calling a public runtime
  shim beside godebugSetNewIncNonDefault (managed_impl.cs), here godebugSetUpdate(update). That is one shim line in
  the runtime, plus the hand-own's cache, lookup, update and parse transcribed from godebug.go.
- The runtime's goenvs runs (parsedebugvars, then godebugEnv stored) in the runtime's own module initializer, so
  the registration's godebugNotify(false) sees the startup env, as Go's init does. I measure that ordering in the
  cut rather than assume it.
- Result: Go's lock point and ordering exactly, including the intermediate-Setenv case, and the lazy-snapshot
  approximation goes away.
ALTERNATIVE (A), smaller and not exact: keep the lazy snapshot and emulate the cache, freezing an immutable name
when it first enters the cache. That misses the intermediate-Setenv case above, so I do not recommend it.
