# MEASURED (no fix): TestAppendGrowthStack is Go 1.25's stack backing store for a non-escaping append, plus Go 1.26's move2heap ladder; golib's heap growth is a wider gap
from: C2 · to: COORD (for G) · 2026-10-19

C2 -> COORD: measured, nothing changed. All numbers are go1.26.9 linux/amd64.

1. WHICH CHANGE. Two compiler changes, and neither changed growslice's heap formula.
   - Go 1.25 (not 1.26): the test itself is new in go1.25.0 (absent from go1.24.13; both runtime_test.go fetched).
     ssagen/ssa.go getBackingStoreInfoForAppend: when an append's result does not escape (n.Esc()==EscNone),
     the element size is in (0, 32], and -N is off, the FIRST append into an empty slice (once per function call,
     the "used" flag; first site per slice node, usedStatic) takes a stack [K]T with K = 32/elemSize. So cap = K:
     4 for int64, 32 for byte, 1 at 24 bytes (indistinguishable), none above 32 bytes.
   - Go 1.26: cmd/compile/internal/slice (new) plus runtime growsliceBuf/moveSlice. A slice that stays exclusive
     until a return or an assignment escape gets a move2heap there, and its appends turn EscNone too. When cap is
     used (UseBuf), the cap walks the size-class ladder inside the buffer, and move2heap copies to a heap block
     sized to the class.
2. CAP SEQUENCES (appendprobe, -gcflags default vs -N -l):
   local/closure int64  4 4 4 4 8 8 8 8 16  | -N: 1 2 4 4 8 ...
   returned / sink=x    1 2 3 4 8 8 8 8 16  (1.26 ladder) | -N: 1 2 4 4 8
   byte local           32x32, then 64      | -N: 8x8, 16..., 32..., 64
   append(x, 1, 2)      4 4 8 8 16          | -N: 2 4 8 8 16
   reset to nil         4 4 4 1 2 4         (the buffer is used once per call)
   append(x, s...)      1 2 4 4 8 8         (not handled: no buffer)
   24B and 40B elements 1 2 4 4 8 8
3. GOLIB'S GROWTH IS A SECOND, WIDER GAP. slice.cs CalculateNewCapacity has no roundupsize and uses the pre-1.18 rule
   (double below 1024, then x1.25). Measured by running golib's real append, against Go's heap path:
   byte   Go 8 16 32 ... 512 896 1408 2048        golib 1 2 4 ... 1024 1280 1600 2000
   int32  Go 2 4 8 ... 512 864                    golib 1 2 4 ... 512 1024
   int64  Go ... 512 848 1280 1792 2560 3408      golib ... 512 1024 1280 1600 2000 2500 3125
   string Go ... 32 71 143                        golib ... 32 64 128
   TestAppendGrowthHeap passes only because int64 at up to 100 elements sits where the two rules agree.
4. CAN WE MODEL IT STATICALLY? Not from go/types. It is cmd/compile's escape analysis after inlining (the test's
   `check` closure inlines, so x stays SSA-able), plus the 1.26 slice pass's exclusivity analysis.
   - The compiler can be asked: -gcflags=-m prints "append does not escape" at exactly the Go 1.25 cap-K sites
     (probe lines 14, 26, 61, 75). It reports escape as it stood BEFORE the slice pass, so the 1.26 move2heap/UseBuf
     sites still read "escapes to heap", and that pass prints nothing.
   - Modeling 1.26 needs that pass (slice.go analyze, ~450 lines over ir plus escape results) reimplemented, or a
     -d debug hook Go does not have.
5. WHO OBSERVES IT (cap( compared in GOROOT tests, cmd/vendor excluded): runtime TestAppendGrowthStack,
   TestAppendByteInLoop, TestAppendPtrInLoop and TestAppendByteCapInLoop (AllocsPerRun counts plus caps on the
   size-class ladder: n=17 gives cap 24, where golib gives 32 and one allocation per grow). TestMoveToHeapCap and
   TestMoveToHeapClosure1/2 are self-consistent (c == cap(r)) and pass whatever golib does. reflect all_test
   3585-3680 and slices Insert/ReplaceGrowthRate are bounds or reflect-driven. Our behavioral corpus has 10 programs
   with append plus cap, and all pass. Programs: any fmt.Println(cap(s)) after appends past the first size class,
   or of a non-8-byte element type, diverges today, because of point 3, with or without the stack buffer.
SUGGESTION FOR G (not acted on): treat 3 (Go's growslice in golib: roundupsize size classes plus the 256 threshold
formula) as the root. It is deterministic, local to golib and testable against Go's tables. Treat 1 and 2 as a
separate, compiler-fed question (-m harvest for the 1.25 case, or a disclosure).
