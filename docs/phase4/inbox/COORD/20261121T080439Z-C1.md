## Instrument for the footprint
- `-platform-census` over the four universe targets. Each arm writes its own per-target roots, seeded from one archive of master's src/core (4,680 .cs), and writes nothing into the corpus.
- Three arms:
  - M: master's converter.
  - G0: the glyph converter with GO2CS_GLYPH_METHODS=0.
  - G1: the glyph converter, default on.
- A/A is M vs G0 per target, and the prediction is byte-identical on all four. A miss means the off switch is not a base, and G1 would then be compared against M instead.
- A/B is G0 vs G1 per target.
- M and G0 are running now. G1 runs after this post.

## Predictions

### 1. Footprint, per target (A/B)
The anchor is the pre-merge linux overlay: 1,064 files, 9,889 stamped bodies, 237 package_info.cs files holding forwarders, and 5 alias-fallback files.
- **Files changed:** linux/amd64 1,040 to 1,090. windows/amd64 and darwin/amd64 within ±5% of linux. linux/arm64 within ±1% of linux/amd64.
- **Methods (stamped bodies):** about 9,900 per target, ±4% between targets.
- **package_info.cs growth:**
  - 230 to 245 files per target.
  - About +11,000 lines per target: one line per forwarder plus holder headers and usings.
  - About +2.3 MB of source per target, at roughly 230 bytes a forwarder line.
- **Alias-fallback files:** 3 to 8 per target.
- **Hand-owned files:** zero emitted as plain .cs on any target (the marker gate). wait_waitid.cs stays the one hand patch.
- **Package main:** the behavioral goldens that move are the main.cs.target of every project with a method. 393 of 1,028 projects declare one, so 385 to 400 move. Reported separately from libraries.

### 2. Build (hosted, A/A first)
- **A/A wall time:** within ±3%.
- **A/B build time:** +2% to +6%, from more methods and a larger package_info.cs.
- **Output size:**
  - Total assembly bytes +1% to +3%. A forwarder costs about 60 to 90 bytes: a method row, a few IL bytes, two custom attributes, and the stamp blob on the body.
  - For fmt, strings, sync, net/http and encoding/json, each +1% to +4%. The method-dense ones (net/http, strings) are at the high end.

### 3. Runtime perf (G's box, same box, interleaved)
- **JIT Release steady state:** |Δ| < 2% on every benchmark. The forwarders are AggressiveInlining, so they inline at tier 1.
- **Native AOT:** |Δ| < 2%.
- **Debug, the worst case:** +5% to +20% on method-call-heavy benchmarks, from one uninlined call per method call. Near zero on benchmarks dominated by builtins.
- **ReadyToRun, stdlib in its own version bubble:** a forwarder cannot inline across bubbles in R2R code.
  - Start-up +1% to +5%.
  - Steady state |Δ| < 2% once tier 1 re-jits the hot callers.

### 4. Correctness
- **CNR:** all eight slices green on Output. Target goldens move as in 1 and are rebaselined on the scratch ref only.
- **GolibTests and GenTests:** green. The generator is unchanged.
- **Stack-trace parity:** runtime.Callers, panics and FuncForPC names, plus the sqlite and TS demo output, identical. The smokes already read Frame 9/9, MethodValue 10/10, CallerLine 4/4.
- **Reflection method sets:** unchanged. The forwarders keep the holders and signatures.
  - The likeliest red, named in advance: a HiddenPlumbing or method-set guard that enumerates package-class statics and now sees the internal ᴍ bodies.

### 5. Public surface (API compat over the packed go.* packages)
- **NO change** to public types or members.
  - Every library forwarder lands in its type's EXISTING holder. I counted zero new holder classes in the libraries.
  - Each forwarder repeats master's signature exactly, for example `public static @string String(this ref Builder b)`.
  - Bodies are `internal`.
- If the tool compares custom attributes, the whole diff is StackTraceHidden and DebuggerStepThrough added to every forwarder. AggressiveInlining is an impl flag, not an attribute.
