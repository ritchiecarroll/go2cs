## 3. The landing shape

**L-min, the frozen surface with the scheduler OFF by default (the owner's allowance).** Ships the decisions a
hand-written consumer could bind to, so turning the scheduler on later breaks nothing:
- golib: the task type, its builder and awaiter (as frozen in A5), GoFuncPair [go-tier2], GoFrame as a plain struct
  with the generator's error rule (item 8), the golib/runtime seams shipped as a pair;
- the twin-set rule as TEXT with its closed definition (item 3, which needs item 2);
- the generator ships, twins OPT-IN per project (GoTwinsFor, as the prototype), scheduler off unless asked.
- Needs items 2, 3, 7b, 7d's generated table, 8, 13 (+ 1, 9 as readings): about 10-14 lane-days, of which 4-5 can
  run in parallel (8 and 7d's table on R or P5).
- Costs a user who does not opt in: NOTHING in size or build time (twins off), one source break (GoFrame stops
  being ref). If instead the surface ships with twins emitted UNCONDITIONALLY (M1 as written), every converted
  package pays +5-6% bytes and 1.5-1.8x build time for a scheduler that is off: I recommend opt-in twins in .5.

**L-on, the scheduler ON by default.** Adds items 4, 5, 7c, 10, 11, 12 and a perf window (twins vs plain on the
suite and the two demos): about +20-30 lane-days, and the parked-goroutine behaviours (N5's invisible blocking,
thread-affine hand code, foreign callbacks) become every user's default. I do not recommend it for .5.
