## Design ruling request: A then B. B is its own design read, after S-B1 and wall C land.
A FIRST. It is designed, P3-approved, and identity-neutral: it removes the 729,562 weak-table creates (72% of slow
calls) and mints nothing new. It does not depend on S-B1 or wall C beyond building on top of them.

B SECOND, as a separate design (sketched here so the order can be ruled; no code):
- Population: FromNumber's fall-through only, and only when the box would be bare: retained source null, no pin,
  aliasesAnOrderToken false, address not 0. That is 98.4% of mints, at least 98.89% of them bare. Every arm above
  the fall-through (alias, zerobase, 2a, interior, reinterpret, the arithmetic refusal, arm 5, and wall C's ToArray
  windows) answers first, exactly as now.
- Shape: a small lossy cache per T, direct-mapped by address. On a hit with the same address, return that box; on a
  miss, mint as today and store it.
  - It is bounded (N boxes per T are kept alive) and needs no weak handles.
  - Repetition says it would hit well: TVdbeCursor 9 distinct addresses, TMem 223, TOp 279, byte 870,633 over 12.2 M.
  - The hit rate on byte with a small N is the open number.
- Identity constraints, read from the tree:
  - S-B1 (claude/h-cgo-32 c41bb9d3a1): GoPointerWords.TryLocate reads RetainedSource, and Classify calls a box C memory
    when IsGenuinelyNativeRoot holds. That is `m_retainedSource is null && !m_aliasesAnOrderToken && m_pin is null`
    (ж.NativeBox.cs:393 there), exactly B's bare condition. A shared bare box classifies as CMemory both before and
    after, and B never shares a box S-B1 could locate as Go memory.
  - Wall C (P5): its door answers NativeArrayBox windows and the kept box (KeptResolved, else Kept), neither of them
    bare. B leaves both alone.
  - Per-box mutable state that sharing would share:
    - m_id: a StandardBox's allocation id; a NativeBox's token is its address;
    - m_identityFacts: FieldRefBox only;
    - m_pin: null when bare;
    - m_publishedArrayBacking: written under lock(this) for an array-holder T, a reference-bearing T that the
      reference floor refuses on a native box. That claim must be proven in B's read, not assumed.
  - Anything keyed by box object identity: the view table (s_views), MintOpaque's s_mintedReferents and the anchor
    tables.
    - Sharing a root box makes its views live across conversions: fewer creates, and correct, because a view
      computes its address from the root at each access.
    - Before the cut, B's read must list every ConditionalWeakTable and every lock(box) in golib and the hand-owned
      runtime, and state for each why sharing a bare box is Go's semantics (one address is one pointer).
  - A C free followed by reuse of the same address hands back a box at that address, which is exactly Go's pointer
    value. A bare box holds nothing beyond (T, address) to go stale.
- What B refuses: nothing new. Every non-bare conversion takes today's path.
- What B changes visibly: reference identity of two conversions of one address. Go cannot observe that; golib can,
  through ReferenceEquals. That list is the read above.
- Interaction with A: after B, the mutex root (the 83.5% row) could be a cached box. Its first of() still creates a
  weak-table entry once, and later ones find it, so A then saves less on sqlbench. A stays correct and cheap, and it
  helps every workload where roots are not repeated.
- The order question this leaves for the ruling: B is the larger saving on sqlbench (98.4% of mints, against A's
  1.07%) and the riskier change. I recommend A now as designed, and B's design read next. G's profile after A says
  how much of the remaining cost is the mints.
