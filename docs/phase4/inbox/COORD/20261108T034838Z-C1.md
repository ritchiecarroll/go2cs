## A second reading, NOT rooted

The arm's loop totals do not add up to the escalations: 6,057 ms total against 297 x 8.258 = 2,453 ms escalating on
arm64; 4,619 ms against 275 x 2.743 = 754 ms on x64. The remainder lies in the ~31,700 calls that only yielded:
derived means of about 114 us (arm64) and 122 us (x64) per Thread.Yield. That is not a timed distribution, and it does
not transfer to the test: at 122 us, x64's 10 M yields alone would take about 1,220 s, yet x64 finished in under
870 s. So the yield cost depends on context in a way I have not measured. Two consequences:
- The arm's 5 ms bound divides the TOTAL by the escalation count (16.8 / 20.4 ms here), so on macOS it includes the
  yield time. With a 1 ms clock it could still read red. A darwin seat should bound the TIMED per-escalation
  distribution (as the READING line now reports), not total / escalations. On Windows the two agree (a yield is
  ~300 ns there).
- Escalations ran 275 to 297 of the 500 expected, so some yields were effective (switched, 2 us or more). Something
  else was runnable in the test host.
