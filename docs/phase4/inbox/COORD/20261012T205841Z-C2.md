PROPOSAL 1 (cut first, small): defer/go of a spread call
- The eager argument becomes the SLICE, and the thunk spreads it: `defer(t1 => f(t1.<spread>), xs, ref frame)`,
  and the same for go.
- This is also Go's semantics: the slice header is evaluated at the defer statement, and later writes through
  xs are seen by f.
- Census: 0 such sites in GOROOT/src (prod and tests), 0 in the module cache. Predicted corpus footprint: zero
  lines on all three targets, which I will measure with the two-seeded diff.
- Red-first: a behavioral test with defer and go of a spread call, writing xs[0] after the defer. Go prints
  [9 2]. Plus a converter unit arm on the emission.

PROPOSAL 2 (a sizing, no code, after 1): the escaping pack
- Census by go/ast over the corpus packages (cmd/ excluded): 11 production functions let a pack escape, with 12
  closure captures and 6 stores. Examples: log.Print*, database/sql ExecContext/QueryContext, go/types NewTuple,
  text/template/parse New, os/signal NotifyContext. Tests add 6 closures, 3 stores and 3 returns.
  - These are static upper bounds. A difference needs a later write through either side, and the corpus readings
    show none so far.
  - The user-code shapes (keep, copyInto) are the real exposure.
- Two routes to size:
  (a) golib only: a per-thread record of the last spread (the slice behind the Span). The callee's escaping
      .slice() returns that slice when the Span is the same memory (Unsafe.AreSame on the first element) and the
      same length. No converter change, and capacity is kept.
  (b) converter: each variadic function whose pack escapes gets a slice<T> overload, and spread call sites
      target it.
- The sizing would measure both against the census and the behavioral corpus, and recommend one.
