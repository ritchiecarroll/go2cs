What is known:
- net's TestLookupNoSuchHost NXDOMAIN subtests fail on darwin on EITHER side:
  - C1-15 x64: Go=fail / C#=pass on 12 keys;
  - the dns read and C1-15 arm64: C#-side keys.
- The text is always "lookup invalid.invalid. on <the image's resolver>:53: read udp ...: i/o timeout", four times
  over the test's 1 s / 5 s / 30 s backoff.
- At rest the same resolver answers invalid.invalid with NXDOMAIN in 5-91 ms on both RIDs, in all three resolver
  modes (run 42513405068's before-probe).
- Go's own test calls testenv.SkipFlakyNet there. So the oracle's own suite treats it as a flaky network, and
  nothing so far implicates a converted path.

The instrument: sweep-shard `ROWS;dnscap=on`, darwin only, never banks.
- tcpdump runs on UDP port 53 for the whole sweep, via pktap with process names where it is allowed.
- A control query (dig MX go2cs-dnscap-control.invalid) proves the capture.
- The summary matches each query to its answer by client endpoint and id, and reports:
  - unanswered counts;
  - rcodes and latency per process, type and name;
  - the unanswered queries in 10 s buckets.
- Checked locally: a synthetic capture (an unanswered NS counted, the control present and absent), 10 option-parser
  cases, YAML, and repoguard.

Dispatch: os-matrix sweep-shard, goos darwin, filter `net;dnscap=on`, both RIDs.

PREDICTION:
- (1) The control query is present on both RIDs. If it is absent on a RID, that RID's capture proves nothing and
  I say so.
- (2) If the NXDOMAIN keys fail on a RID this read: that RID shows invalid.invalid MX/NS/TXT queries to the
  resolver that went UNANSWERED (no response packet at all), clustered in time, not answered late. So the cause is
  the resolver dropping queries under the sweep's load, on whichever process asked.
  The outcome that refutes this: NXDomain responses do arrive for the failing queries (late, or on time), and the
  client still times out. That would put the cause in the client, Go's or ours.
- (3) The failure is intermittent, and the keys may pass on one or both RIDs this read. In that case the capture
  still gives the unanswered rate under load, and I do not claim a root from a clean read.
